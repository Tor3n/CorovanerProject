# World, societies, and caravans

**Derived requirements and coworker guidance.** Based on the creator's world-setting
passage added to [vision.md](vision.md) on 2026-10-21. The source remains verbatim;
this document organizes its meaning for writing, art, and gameplay. Suggested
applications below are proposals, not additional canon or implemented systems.

## Requirements derived from the creator's passage

### A distant End-day with no established explanation

The player enters a world long after the End-day. At the start, nobody explains
what happened or provides a reliable account; so much time has passed that people
have probably forgotten. A nuclear war or war with AI are possibilities mentioned
by the creator, not selected history. The catastrophe's effects have worn away
with time. Its precise cause, date, and any eventual discovery remain unresolved.

There is **no magic and there are no mutants**. Human cruelty supplies the threat
expressed by “Humans are worse then mutants.” Robots and advanced machinery
belong to the setting's technology; their existence does not establish an AI war
as the cause of the End-day. Religious belief does not establish supernatural facts.

### Farming societies and fragmented political life

Most society is poor and agricultural. People work the land by hand or with
machines. Some communities deliberately seek a wholly medieval way of life and
sometimes succeed. Advanced factions also exist; technology is distributed very
unevenly across the world.

The wider world contains thousands of small nations or principalities. They trade,
fight, and intermarry. The creator's comparisons to Rus before unification and
fragmented Germany describe political fragmentation, not a requirement to reproduce
their exact history, peoples, or institutions.

The earlier vision still calls for a playable map on the scale of a small country.
How many of the world's states appear on that map is open. Thousands of states in
the setting does not, by itself, require thousands of simulated factions on screen.

### Caravans, routes, and unequal transport

The main story concerns caravans and trade: finding the best way through deserts,
scarce forests, industrial wastelands, and great city ruins. Exploration and
commercial travel belong at the center of both the personal journey and the later
caravan empire.

| Setting element | Creator-established direction | Detail still open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rural caravans | Frontier-western character, oxen, horses, and memorable wagons. | Wagon designs, carrying capacity, animal care, and travel rules. |
| Advanced transport | More advanced civilizations use cars; some use railways, which are scarce. | Fuel, maintenance, ownership, network extent, and who can use them. |
| Electric propulsion and drones | Some societies use them. | Vehicle roles, energy sources, range, and availability to the player. |
| Hostile territory | Strangers and their vehicles or drones may be fired upon. | Local laws, recognition, warning, permission, and encounter mechanics. |
| Air defence | Anti-aircraft guns and other contraptions are developed in this timeline. | Designs, distribution, effectiveness, and how the player learns about threats. |

Advanced transport is part of this politically divided world. Its presence must
not silently make borders, local hostility, or the creator's air-defence premise
irrelevant. Exact balancing and whether the player directly operates any vehicle
remain design questions.

### Robots and beliefs

Semi-sentient robots and hive-mind robots exist alongside humans and advanced
factions. Their origins, degree of autonomy, organization, relationships with
people, and possible roles in trade are not yet defined. Do not assume all robots
are hostile, share one mind, or belong to a single faction.

Religions must vary. The creator names Amish-like communities, belief in a
singularity, dangerous cults, and people who want to modify their bodies with
metal. These establish a range of beliefs and aspirations, not named religions,
one universal doctrine, or approved powers. Whether these groups overlap, what
modifications are achievable, and how a faith leader gains authority remain open.

## Proposed applications for collaborators

The following are ways to express the requirements, not newly settled lore:

- **Show long habitation.** Compose fields, maintained routes, repairs, and reused
  structures beside old ruins. Let present-day livelihoods make the world feel
  lived in across generations. Avoid making fresh blast damage or universal
  radioactive decay the default explanation for every landscape.
- **Give journeys local context.** A road's value might depend on markets,
  crossings, trust, conflict, or access to repairs as well as distance. A border
  can be a place to meet people and learn customs, not only a combat trigger.
- **Keep low-tech societies viable.** Depict skill, productive land, and social
  organization where they succeed. High technology need not imply moral virtue,
  political unity, or a universally superior way of life.
- **Separate social dimensions.** Describe a community's livelihood, government,
  beliefs, technology, and treatment of strangers independently. Avoid reducing
  every religious group to a technology tier or making every cult and every
  body-modification movement the same faction.
- **Make rural caravans recognizable.** Vary wagon silhouettes, coverings, loads,
  harness, and repairs at the actual map scale. More elaborate character assets
  should not displace the map's visual priority.
- **Let uncertainty remain uncertainty.** Contradictory stories could be local
  beliefs; any reliable evidence or definitive explanation of the End-day would
  need a separate creative decision. Solving it is not an established main quest.

## Boundaries and follow-up

The setting expands the existing vision; it does not replace the sudden RPG-to-4X
transition, the fixed-camera requirement, or the largely static landscape. Trade,
politics, and religion must be developed within those constraints. No named
factions, borders, resource simulation, vehicle progression, or new runtime systems
are approved by this document.
